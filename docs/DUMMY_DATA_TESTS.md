a) The acceleration
b) The distance traveled

### Question 6 (Essay - 65 marks)
**Question**: Explain Newton's Three Laws of Motion with real-life examples. Include diagrams where necessary.

**Expected Answer**:
**First Law (Law of Inertia)**: An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an external force.
Example: A book on a table remains at rest until someone picks it up.

**Second Law (F = ma)**: The force acting on an object is equal to its mass times its acceleration.
Example: Pushing a shopping cart - more force is needed to accelerate a heavier cart.

**Third Law (Action-Reaction)**: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
Example: When you jump, you push the ground down (action), and the ground pushes you up (reaction).

### Question 4 (Short Answer - 10 marks)
**Question**: Differentiate between plant cells and animal cells (list 5 differences).

**Expected Answer**:

2. Plant cells have chloroplasts; animal cells do not

### Question 5 (Essay - 16 marks)
**Question**: Describe the structure and function of the nucleus in detail.

**Expected Answer**:
The nucleus is the control center of the cell, often called the "brain" of the cell. It is surrounded by a double membrane called the nuclear envelope, which has pores for material exchange. Inside the nucleus, we find:

1. **Chromatin**: DNA and proteins that condense to form chromosomes during cell division
2. **Nucleolus**: Site of ribosome production
3. **Nuclear sap**: Fluid that fills the nucleus

Functions:
- Controls all cellular activities
- Stores genetic information (DNA)
- Regulates gene expression
- Coordinates cell growth and reproduction
- Transmits genetic information to offspring

The nucleus is essential for cell survival and cannot be removed without causing cell death.
